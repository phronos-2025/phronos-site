# Research Agenda

This document translates Phronos research goals into testable hypotheses, organized by theoretical foundation. Each hypothesis is stated with dependencies, evidence requirements, and confidence assessments.

Our research program investigates whether stable patterns of cognition—"cognitive phenotypes"—can be reliably measured from behavioral traces in human-AI interaction data. This agenda makes explicit what we believe, what we've tested, and what remains uncertain.

---

## Reading This Document

**Status Values:**
- **Supported** — Library article establishes moderate-to-strong evidence
- **Partial** — Some evidence exists with significant caveats
- **Gap** — Identified as needed but no direct evidence
- **Untested** — Empirical question requiring new research

**Notation:**
- **H[n]** — Hypothesis number (sequential within article)
- **→** — Depends on
- **⇒** — Unlocks/enables

## Foundational Claims

These claims underlie the entire research program. They must be defensible before downstream work proceeds.

<section id="F1">

### F1: Cognitive Phenotypes Are Real and Measurable

**Claim:** Stable patterns of cognition—"cognitive phenotypes"—exist as individual differences that can be reliably measured from behavioral traces in human-AI interaction data.

**Unpacked:**
- Cognitive phenotypes are not artifacts of measurement
- They reflect genuine variation in how people think, not just how they write
- They are stable enough to be meaningful (not purely situational)
- They can be distinguished from LLM-generated patterns

**Evidence needed:**
- Convergent validity with established cognitive measures
- Discriminant validity from linguistic style alone
- Test-retest reliability across sessions
- Distinguishability from LLM outputs on same tasks

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Low-to-Moderate  
**Library coverage:** [LIB-001](/library/linguistic-markers) (semantic networks), [LIB-002](/library/digital-validity) (stability gap)  
**Key gap:** Test-retest reliability ([H2.3](#H2.3))

</section>

<section id="F2">

### F2: Chat Data Reveals Cognition, Not Just Communication

**Claim:** What people type in conversations with AI systems encodes cognitive processes (reasoning, association, belief structures) beyond mere communicative intent.

**Unpacked:**
- Text is a window into thought, not just social performance
- The cognitive signal is extractable despite communicative noise
- Human-AI interaction reduces some social filtering present in human-human text

**Evidence needed:**
- Correlation between chat-derived measures and cognitive task performance
- Demonstration that chat features predict outcomes beyond self-report
- Analysis of what chat captures that surveys miss (and vice versa)

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Low-to-Moderate  
**Library coverage:** [LIB-001](/library/linguistic-markers), [LIB-002](/library/digital-validity)  
**Key gap:** Task-naturalistic convergence ([H2.6](#H2.6))

</section>

## LIB-001: Linguistic Markers of Cognition

**Question:** What features of language reliably encode cognitive processes, and which are measurable at scale?

**Library Confidence:** Low-to-Moderate

<section id="H1.1">

### H1.1: Pronoun Patterns Encode Psychological States

First-person pronoun usage patterns (frequency, shifts, I/we ratio) correlate with measurable psychological states including self-focus, distress, and relational orientation.

**Status:** Supported  
**Confidence:** High  
**Evidence:** LIWC tradition, clinical validation studies

</section>

<section id="H1.2">

### H1.2: Semantic Network Structure Varies Across Individuals

The structure of associations between concepts in memory—measurable through word associations or embedding distances—shows stable individual differences that correlate with cognitive style and creative ability.

**Status:** Supported  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Evidence:** Kenett et al. 2014 (network topology predicts creativity), Beaty & Kenett 2023 (theoretical synthesis), Kenett et al. 2018 (flexibility of thought)  
**Limitations:** Most studies use small samples; brief tasks may not capture full structure  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H1.3">

### H1.3: Semantic Networks Can Be Assessed via Chat

An individual's semantic network structure can be inferred from their chat behavior—prompt content, associative patterns, topic transitions—without explicit association tasks.

**Status:** Gap  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence needed:** Correlation between chat-derived and task-derived semantic measures  
**Dependency:** → [H2.6](#H2.6)

</section>

<section id="H1.4">

### H1.4: Chat Captures Identity Constructs

Self-concept, values, and beliefs can be reliably measured from chat interactions, with validity comparable to or exceeding traditional self-report instruments.

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence needed:** Convergent/discriminant validity studies  
**Dependency:** → [F1](#F1), [F2](#F2)

</section>

<section id="H1.5">

### H1.5: Chat Captures Personality

Big Five personality traits and related constructs can be reliably inferred from chat interaction patterns.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Moderate (for text generally), Low (for chat specifically)  
**Evidence:** Some validation of text-based personality inference  
**Gap:** Chat-specific validation

</section>

<section id="H1.6">

### H1.6: Chat Captures Reasoning Style

Individual differences in reasoning (analytical vs. intuitive, logical structure, metacognitive accuracy) are detectable from chat interaction patterns.

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence needed:** Correlation with reasoning tasks, think-aloud protocols

</section>

<section id="H1.7">

### H1.7: Embedding Validity for Semantic Distance

Semantic distances computed from word embeddings correlate with human judgments of semantic similarity and predict performance on semantic tasks.

**Status:** Partial (population-level supported; individual-level untested)  
**Confidence:** Low-to-Moderate  
**Evidence:** Hill et al. 2015 (SimLex-999, r ≈ 0.44-0.56 for aggregate judgments), Auguste et al. 2017 (priming RT prediction at population level)  
**Critical caveat:** Validated for population-level semantics only. Individual-difference validity untested.  
**Empirical finding:** Different embedding models produce substantially different scores (GloVe vs. OpenAI: r = 0.60; 20.7-point systematic difference). Scores are model-dependent.  
**Limitation:** Cultural/linguistic bias in embeddings  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H1.8">

### H1.8: Divergent Thinking via Semantic Spread

Divergent thinking—the capacity to generate varied responses to open prompts—is measurable via mean pairwise semantic distance and correlates with broader creative ability.

**Status:** Supported (for 10-word unconstrained task); transfer to brief constrained format calibrated but not validated  
**Confidence:** Moderate (with transfer caveat)  
**Evidence:** Olson et al. 2021 (r = 0.40 with composite creativity, r = 0.40 with AUT, r = 0.28 with RAT; N = 8,914)  
**Critical caveat:** Original DAT uses 10 words unconstrained; INS-001 uses 2-5 words under constraint.  
**Empirical finding:** INS-001.1 produces 8.6 points lower spread than DAT; INS-001.2 produces 8.0 points lower. Gap is documented but not validated against external creativity criteria.  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H1.9">

### H1.9: Constraint-Construct Relationship

Task constraints (free vs. goal-directed association, relevance requirements, communicability demands) determine which aspect of semantic cognition is measured. Different constraint structures yield related but distinct constructs.

**Status:** Supported  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Evidence:** Beaty & Kenett 2023 (free vs. goal-directed distinction), Merseal et al. 2025 (artists exceed scientists in free but not goal-directed association)  
**Empirical finding:** Constraint effects are large (Cohen's d = 1.02 between unconstrained DAT and constrained INS-001.2).  
**Design implication:** INS-001.1 and INS-001.2 measure related but distinct constructs  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

## LIB-002: Digital Validity

**Question:** Under what conditions can digital interactions serve as valid proxies for cognitive states?

**Library Confidence:** Low

<section id="H2.1">

### H2.1: Digital Tasks Have Ecological Validity

Cognitive abilities measured through digital games and tasks predict performance on traditional laboratory assessments, establishing that digital contexts can yield valid cognitive measurement.

**Status:** Supported (for memory/attention); untested for creativity  
**Confidence:** Moderate (domain-limited)  
**Evidence:** Pedersen et al. 2023 (N = 10,725; game-based measures correlate with traditional tests; 5x faster)  
**Critical caveat:** Validated for memory/attention/processing speed, not creativity/semantic constructs. We are extrapolating precedent without direct validation.

</section>

<section id="H2.2">

### H2.2: Anonymity Alters Disclosure

Anonymous chat interactions elicit different—potentially more authentic—self-disclosure than identified interactions.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Evidence:** Online disinhibition literature

</section>

<section id="H2.3">

### H2.3: Measurements Are Stable Within Individuals

Cognitive phenotype measurements from chat show acceptable test-retest reliability (r > 0.7) across measurement occasions separated by meaningful time intervals.

**Status:** Gap (Critical)  
**Confidence:** Unknown  
**Evidence needed:** Longitudinal measurement study with INS-001  
**Note:** No published test-retest data for DAT or semantic spread measures.  
**Empirical acknowledgment:** Single-trial variance is high; multiple trials and aggregates are recommended. This addresses precision, not construct stability.  
**Priority:** Critical — blocks trait interpretation of INS-001

</section>

<section id="H2.4">

### H2.4: Measurements Generalize Across Platforms

Cognitive phenotypes identified in one AI system (e.g., ChatGPT) are detectable in interactions with other systems (Claude, Gemini) and modalities (voice, code).

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Unknown  
**Evidence needed:** Cross-platform validation study

</section>

<section id="H2.5">

### H2.5: Selection Effects Are Characterizable

The population that generates chat data (particularly power users) differs systematically from the general population in ways that can be measured and adjusted for.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** High (that differences exist), Low (that they're characterized)  
**Evidence:** Pedersen et al. 2023 (sample gender skew), Thompson et al. 2020 (cultural/linguistic bias)

</section>

<section id="H2.6">

### H2.6: Task-Naturalistic Convergence

Task-based semantic assessments (structured prompts with embedding-scored responses) and naturalistic chat-derived measures of the same constructs show convergent validity.

**Status:** Gap  
**Confidence:** Low (hypothesis with zero supporting evidence)  
**Evidence needed:** Convergent validity between task-based (INS-001) and chat-derived measures  
**Note:** No published studies compare these modalities for semantic constructs. If task and naturalistic measures diverge, INS-001 measures task performance rather than cognitive style.  
**Priority:** High — determines measurement strategy  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H2.7">

### H2.7: AI Interaction Doesn't Invalidate Measurement

The presence of AI as interlocutor, judge, or baseline in cognitive assessment doesn't systematically distort measurement of the target construct.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence:** Vicente & Matute 2023 (AI bias inheritance demonstrated for classification); Cazalets & Dambre 2025, Stephenson et al. 2024 (human-AI comparison methodologies exist)  
**Gap:** Reflexivity effects demonstrated for bias, not for creativity/semantic measures  
**Concern:** Unknown if AI interaction affects divergent thinking specifically  
**Note:** Human validation pending. Current scoring uses Claude Haiku 4.5 as standardized reference; human-AI divergence unquantified.  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1 (Haiku as guesser), INS-001.2 (Haiku as baseline)

</section>

<section id="H2.8">

### H2.8: Task Constraints Affect Spread Measurement

Task constraints systematically affect measured semantic spread, requiring calibration when comparing across task formats.

**Status:** Supported (empirically documented)  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Empirical finding:** DAT pairwise distances vs. INS-001.2 bridging clue distances: Cohen's d = 1.02 (large effect). Constraint genuinely compresses associative range; calibration assumption not validated.  
**Implication:** Scores from different task formats are not directly comparable without calibration  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

## LIB-003: Reflexive Identity

**Question:** How do AI interactions shape cognition while purporting to measure it?

<section id="H3.1">

### H3.1: AI Interaction Alters Reasoning

Extended interaction with LLMs changes measurable aspects of human reasoning—potentially including analytical depth, source reliance, or metacognitive calibration.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence:** Vicente & Matute 2023 (bias inheritance persists after AI removed)  
**Evidence needed:** Pre-post or longitudinal design for reasoning specifically  
**Dependency:** → [H1.6](#H1.6)

</section>

<section id="H3.2">

### H3.2: AI Interaction Alters Identity

Extended interaction with LLMs changes measurable aspects of self-concept, values, or beliefs—beyond what would occur through other information sources.

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence needed:** Pre-post or longitudinal design with control condition  
**Dependency:** → [H1.4](#H1.4)

</section>

## LIB-008: Instrument Design

**Question:** How can cognitive instruments be designed to be engaging, valid, and interpretable without causing harm?

**Library Confidence:** Low-to-Moderate

<section id="H8.1">

### H8.1: Game-Based Assessment Validity

Game-based and gamified cognitive assessment formats maintain measurement validity while increasing engagement and reducing assessment time.

**Status:** Supported (for memory/attention); creativity-specific gamification not validated  
**Confidence:** Moderate (domain-limited)  
**Evidence:** Pedersen et al. 2023 (8 abilities, 5x faster, convergent validity), Lumsden et al. 2016 (systematic review of 33 tasks)  
**Critical caveat:** Validated for memory/attention, not creativity/divergent thinking. INS-001 represents extrapolation.  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H8.2">

### H8.2: Word Association Games Reveal Cognition

Word association games—requiring generation of clues connecting concepts—reveal semantic memory structure and associative processes in measurable ways.

**Status:** Supported  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Evidence:** Kumar et al. 2021 (Connector game; associative models predict performance), Cazalets & Dambre 2025 (Word Synchronization Challenge), Stephenson et al. 2024 (Codenames benchmark), Xu et al. 2025 (CK-Arena)  
**Limitation:** Games designed for entertainment may elicit different processes than assessment  
**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H8.3">

### H8.3: Task Constraints Affect Measured Constructs

Design parameters—clue count, time pressure, explicit constraints—systematically affect what constructs are measured and how scores should be interpreted.

**Status:** Supported (empirically documented)  
**Confidence:** Moderate  
**Empirical findings:**
- Optimal clue counts: INS-001.1 = 2 clues; INS-001.2 = 4 clues
- Relevance-spread redundancy required metric redesign (r = -0.77 in INS-001.1, r = -0.61 in INS-001.2). Solution: replaced relevance with fidelity (r = +0.06 with spread).
- Constraint calibration vs. validation: documented but construct preservation assumed, not tested  

**Instruments:** INS-001.1, INS-001.2

</section>

<section id="H8.4">

### H8.4: Results Can Be Presented Without Harm

Cognitive assessment results can be presented to participants in ways that inform without causing psychological harm.

**Status:** Gap (Critical)  
**Confidence:** Unknown  
**Evidence needed:** Study of psychological effects of creativity assessment feedback  
**Concern:** Labeling effects, misinterpretation as stable trait, comparison anxiety. Creativity may be more identity-linked than memory or attention.  
**Priority:** High (ethical obligation)  
**Note:** Until studied, use precautionary approach: avoid trait language, frame as snapshot, provide context

</section>

<section id="H8.5">

### H8.5: Self-Location Is Possible

Individuals can meaningfully locate themselves within distributions of cognitive traits using appropriately designed instruments.

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Moderate (the goal is achievable)  
**Dependency:** → [H8.1](#H8.1), [H8.4](#H8.4)

</section>

<section id="H8.6">

### H8.6: Phenotypes Associate with Performance

Cognitive phenotypes measured from chat predict performance (task success relative to peers) on external criteria.

**Status:** Partial  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Evidence:** Said-Metwaly et al. 2024 (DT-CA meta-analysis r = 0.18)  
**Note:** Effect sizes are modest; creativity measures explain ~3% of creative achievement variance  
**Evidence needed:** INS-001-specific predictive validity studies

</section>

<section id="H8.7">

### H8.7: Phenotypes Associate with Fitness

Cognitive phenotypes measured from chat correlate with cognitive fitness measures.

**Status:** Untested  
**Confidence:** Low  
**Dependency:** → H5.3, H5.4 (fitness framework)

</section>

<section id="H8.8">

### H8.8: Phenotype Change Is Measurable

Changes in cognitive phenotype over time can be reliably detected, distinguishing true change from measurement error.

**Status:** Gap  
**Confidence:** Unknown  
**Evidence needed:** Longitudinal tracking with reliability assessment  
**Dependency:** → [H2.3](#H2.3)

</section>

## Joint Confidence Assessment

Individual hypothesis confidence ratings treat claims as independent. When combined for instrument development (e.g., INS-001 depends on LIB-001, LIB-002, and LIB-008), joint confidence is substantially lower.

**Key compounding uncertainties for INS-001:**

| Component | Hypothesis | Status | Confidence |
|-----------|------------|--------|------------|
| Embedding validity for individuals | [H1.7](#H1.7) | Partial | Low-to-Moderate |
| Brief-task validity | [H1.8](#H1.8) | Calibrated, not validated | Moderate (with caveat) |
| Test-retest reliability | [H2.3](#H2.3) | Gap | Unknown |
| Creativity-game transfer | [H8.1](#H8.1) | Domain extrapolation | Moderate (domain-limited) |

**Joint interpretation:** Until these are independently validated, INS-001 confidence should be interpreted as **Low** despite moderate ratings on component claims.
